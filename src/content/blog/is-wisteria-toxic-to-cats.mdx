## Quick Answer: Is Wisteria Toxic to Cats?

Yes. Wisteria is toxic to cats. ASPCA lists wisteria as toxic to dogs, cats, and horses, with lectin and wisterin glycoside as toxic principles. Cats that chew or swallow wisteria leaves, vines, flowers, seeds, or pods may develop vomiting, diarrhea, depression, and other signs of stomach upset.

Seeds and seed pods deserve special caution because they may contain more concentrated plant material and can be chewed open. If your cat ate wisteria, call your veterinarian or a pet poison hotline with the plant name, part eaten, time, and symptoms.

## Why Wisteria Matters in Cat Homes

Cats are less likely than dogs to eat large amounts of garden debris, but they still chew plants. Indoor cats may nibble potted vines, outdoor cats may investigate fallen pods, and curious kittens may mouth leaves or flowers.

The danger is not only the visible flower. Wisteria vines, seed pods, and plant debris can all create access points. If a cat chews a pod and then grooms residue from paws or fur, the exposure may be hard to estimate.

Wisteria is not in the same emergency category as true lilies, which can cause kidney failure in cats, or sago palm, which can cause severe liver injury. But it is still a toxic plant and should not be kept where cats can chew it.

## Symptoms of Wisteria Poisoning in Cats

Possible signs include:

- Vomiting
- Vomit that may contain blood
- Diarrhea
- Drooling or nausea
- Depression or hiding
- Reduced appetite
- Weakness
- Abdominal discomfort

Cats often hide illness, so subtle behavior changes matter. A cat that hides, refuses food, vomits more than once, seems weak, or may have swallowed a seed pod should be handled more urgently than a cat that only sniffed the plant.

For broader warning signs, use [signs your cat is sick](/blog/signs-your-cat-is-sick) and the [poisoning symptoms guide](/toxicity/symptoms).

## What to Do If Your Cat Ate Wisteria

1. Move your cat away from the plant.
2. Prevent more chewing and remove loose pods, seeds, or leaves.
3. Take photos of the plant, flowers, leaves, pods, and any missing pieces.
4. Note the time and symptoms.
5. Call your veterinarian, an emergency clinic, ASPCA Animal Poison Control, or Pet Poison Helpline.
6. Do not induce vomiting unless a veterinary professional gives that instruction.

If your cat is repeatedly vomiting, passing blood, hiding, weak, shaking, breathing abnormally, or refusing food after exposure, seek urgent veterinary guidance.

## How Wisteria Compares With Other Toxic Plants for Cats

Wisteria is toxic, but cat owners should also know the high-emergency plant list:

- [True lilies and daylilies](/blog/lily-toxicity-cats): kidney-failure emergency in cats.
- [Sago palm](/blog/sago-palm-toxicity-pets): severe liver and neurologic risk.
- Oleander, foxglove, and lily of the valley: cardiac risk.
- Pothos, philodendron, dieffenbachia, and peace lily: oral irritation from oxalate crystals.

For a wider list, read [household plants toxic to cats](/blog/household-plants-toxic-to-cats).

## Prevention for Cat Households

The safest plan is to keep wisteria out of cat-accessible spaces. If wisteria is already outside:

- Do not bring cut wisteria indoors.
- Keep indoor cats away from open patios with fallen pods.
- Pick up seed pods before cats or dogs investigate them.
- Use barriers around vines and garden beds.
- Offer safe cat grass or enrichment so cats are less likely to chew plants.
- Photograph unknown plants before calling the vet so identification is easier.

If you are replacing risky plants, start with the [cat toxicity guide](/toxicity/cats) and check any plant before bringing it into the home.

## FAQ

### Is wisteria poisonous to cats?

Yes. Wisteria is listed as toxic to cats. Seeds, pods, leaves, vines, and flowers should not be eaten.

### What happens if a cat eats wisteria?

A cat that eats wisteria may vomit, have diarrhea, drool, hide, seem depressed, or lose appetite. Call a veterinarian or poison hotline with details about the exposure.

### Are wisteria flowers safe for cats?

No part of wisteria should be treated as safe for cats to eat. Flowers may be less commonly eaten than seeds or pods, but ingestion is still a concern.

### Should cat owners remove wisteria?

If your cat can reach the plant, fallen pods, or cut stems, removal or secure exclusion is the safer choice. Cats should not have access to toxic plant material.
