## Quick Answer: Is Wisteria Poisonous to Dogs?

Yes. Wisteria is poisonous to dogs. The plant is listed as toxic to dogs, cats, and horses, and the most concerning parts are the seeds and seed pods. Exposure can cause vomiting, sometimes with blood, diarrhea, depression, and other gastrointestinal signs.

If your dog ate wisteria, do not wait to see whether symptoms become severe. Move your dog away from the plant, collect a photo or sample, estimate what part was eaten, and call your veterinarian, an emergency clinic, or a pet poison hotline.

You can also open the direct [wisteria toxicity record](/toxicity/wisteria) for a fast dog and cat risk check.

## Why Wisteria Is Risky for Dogs

Wisteria vines are common on fences, pergolas, trellises, and garden walls. The flowers are easy to notice, but dog exposures often involve chewing vines, fallen plant material, seeds, or pods.

The ASPCA lists wisteria as toxic and identifies lectin and wisterin glycoside as toxic principles. In practical terms, the main risk for dog owners is gastrointestinal poisoning after chewing or swallowing plant material, especially seeds or pods.

Some dogs are at higher risk because they chew plants out of boredom, mouth garden debris, dig around roots, or eat fallen pods before an owner notices.

## Symptoms of Wisteria Poisoning in Dogs

Possible signs include:

- Vomiting
- Vomit that may contain blood
- Diarrhea
- Drooling or nausea
- Depression or unusual tiredness
- Reduced appetite
- Abdominal discomfort
- Weakness or behavior change

Symptoms can vary by the part eaten, the amount, the dog's size, and whether the dog has other medical issues. A small puppy that chews seed pods deserves more caution than a large dog that briefly mouthed a leaf and spit it out.

Use the [poisoning symptoms guide](/toxicity/symptoms) if you are comparing vomiting, diarrhea, weakness, shaking, collapse, breathing changes, or other urgent signs.

## What to Do If Your Dog Ate Wisteria

1. Move your dog away from the plant.
2. Remove loose pods, seeds, leaves, or vines from the area.
3. Take photos of the plant and the part your dog chewed.
4. Estimate when the exposure happened and how much may be missing.
5. Call your veterinarian, an emergency clinic, ASPCA Animal Poison Control, or Pet Poison Helpline.
6. Do not induce vomiting unless a veterinary professional tells you to.

If your dog is already vomiting repeatedly, weak, depressed, passing bloody diarrhea, shaking, or unable to stay alert, treat it as urgent.

## When an Emergency Vet Visit May Be Needed

Your veterinarian may recommend an exam based on the amount eaten, plant part, dog size, time since exposure, and symptoms. Emergency care may include anti-nausea medication, fluids, monitoring, or other treatment depending on the case.

## How to Prevent Wisteria Poisoning

The safest option is to keep wisteria out of dog-accessible areas. If the plant is already established:

- Fence off the vine and root area.
- Pick up fallen seed pods quickly.
- Do not let puppies chew garden debris.
- Avoid composting pods where dogs can dig.
- Train a reliable "leave it" cue, but do not rely on training alone.
- Replace accessible toxic plants with clearly pet-safer options.

## FAQ

### Are wisteria seeds poisonous to dogs?

Yes. Wisteria seeds and pods are especially concerning because dogs may chew or swallow them. Call your veterinarian or a poison hotline if your dog ate seeds or pods.

### What happens if a dog eats wisteria?

Wisteria exposure can cause vomiting, diarrhea, depression, nausea, and possible blood in vomit. The safest response is to call for veterinary guidance instead of waiting for symptoms to progress.

### Is a little wisteria leaf dangerous for dogs?

Any known ingestion deserves caution because wisteria is listed as toxic. Risk depends on the amount, plant part, dog size, and symptoms, so call your veterinarian with details.

### Should I remove wisteria if I have a dog?

If your dog chews plants or has access to fallen seed pods, removal or secure exclusion is the safest choice. At minimum, block access and remove pods quickly.
